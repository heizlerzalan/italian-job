Geography
Geography: The City of Water Treviso is situated on a low-lying plain, roughly 15 meters above sea level. It sits at the confluence of several rivers, which defines its character and urban layout. The River System: The Sile and Botteniga rivers are the city's lifeblood. The Botteniga divides into several canals (cagnani) that flow through the historic center, giving the city its "Little Venice" aesthetic. Topography: To the south and east, the land is flat and fertile, dominated by agriculture. To the north, the terrain begins to ripple into the Prosecco Hills (a UNESCO World Heritage site) before hitting the dramatic peaks of the Dolomite Mountains. Hydrogeology: The area is famous for its risorgive (resurgence springs), where groundwater naturally bubbles up to the surface, keeping the surrounding landscape lush and green year-round.
Climate: Humid Subtropical Treviso experiences a humid subtropical climate, characterized by four distinct seasons and significant humidity throughout the year. Winter (December – February) Winters are cold and often damp. Temperatures typically hover between 0°C and 7°C. One of the most defining features of a Treviso winter is the dense fog, caused by temperature inversions and stagnant air common in the Po Valley. Spring (March – May) This is a mild but unpredictable transition period. While rainfall is frequent, the rising temperatures turn the surrounding countryside and the Prosecco hills a vibrant green. Summer (June – August) Summers are hot and can be quite sultry. Highs often reach between 28°C and 32°C. The humidity can make it feel much warmer, though the heat is frequently broken by sudden, heavy afternoon thunderstorms. Autumn (September – November) Autumn is generally cool and crisp. It is widely considered the most pleasant time of year as the air clears, the humidity drops, and the landscape changes color during the vineyard harvest.
